Automating debt recovery outreach

Collections agencies work large balances by hand and leave small ones alone, because paying a person to chase them costs more than they return. We built a CRM that ran the outreach itself. Regulatory limits on first-time outbound calling closed off the main channel, and the company wound down.

Small balances Automating the outreach lowered the cost of working an account far enough that low-value debt became viable to pursue, which manual collections could not justify.

Problem

Debt portfolio sellers place accounts with agencies that work them through automated email and human outreach. Large balances justify that labour and small ones do not, so the tail of low-value accounts goes largely unworked.

Decision

Automate the outreach rather than staff it. If sequencing, follow-up and channel switching ran without a person, the cost of working an account would fall far enough to make small balances worth pursuing.

Implementation

We built a custom CRM with a student engineering team and connected it to automation and language tooling. Outreach ran as sequences across email, voicemail and phone calls, advanced by time-based and activity-based triggers, so an account moved through follow-up on its own and stopped when the debtor responded.

My contribution

I co-founded the company, set the thesis and the outreach design, and took the model to debt portfolio sellers directly.

Outcome

Regulatory limits on first-time outbound calling closed off the channel the sequence depended on, and the company wound down. The underlying idea — that automation changes which accounts are worth working — is one I still think was right.

This entry is described from my own recollection and the working materials I retain. It carries no performance figures, because the operating records stayed with the company.
